# Assessments, study materials, and projects

Read this reference when supplied materials contain assessments, study guides, practice packets, answer keys, substantial assignments, project instructions, or rubrics. Use the actual documents as the basis for academic advice.

## Assessment record

For each distinct assessment, extract the student, subject, assessment name, module/chapter/unit, scheduled date and tentative status, covered material, required skills, teacher instructions, review resources, and useful study priorities. Record eligibility and special conditions where supplied, such as a retest threshold, permitted notes, grade cap, or alternative schedule. Do not transfer one assessment's rules to another.

Compare accessible earlier reports to determine whether an assessment is newly announced, rescheduled, canceled, past its scheduled date, or associated with a new unit. A past scheduled date is not proof of completion. Report scores, grades, feedback, or actual outcomes only when school records provide them. Curriculum descriptions alone do not establish the child's performance.

If dates disagree, list every relevant date and source. Explain which statement is more directly supported and why, preserve unresolved uncertainty, and recommend confirming the schedule. Preparing by the earliest plausible date can be a labeled provisional recommendation. An explicit correction may settle an earlier claim; mere recency cannot establish cancellation.

## Read the study materials

Inspect the complete packet, including images, diagrams, units, numbering, selected-response choices, written responses, worked examples, and teacher annotations. Identify:

- Concepts, vocabulary, formulas, procedures, and prerequisite skills actually present.
- Question types, multiple-choice versus written-response sections, and recurring problem-solving patterns.
- What the student must be able to do, including explanations, representations, calculations, or evidence where required.
- Especially challenging concepts and plausible mistakes, labeled as suggested preparation rather than evidence the child is struggling.
- School-provided practice and available answer keys, plus material gaps.

Translate coverage into specific study actions. For example, if the supplied review includes mixed-unit metric measurement, recommend converting kilograms/grams, regrouping mixed units, and solving the actual kinds of word problems present. Do not add coverage from a generic grade-level curriculum or infer that every page of a practice packet will be tested.

In the assessment explanation, state the observed question formats (for example, selected response and short explanations) and cover the range of skills in the supplied review rather than only its first questions. Distinguish the review's formats from confirmed test formats when the teacher has not specified the latter.

Keep **Required homework**, **Optional school-provided practice**, and **Suggested home practice** separate. An answer key alone does not supply the missing questions or establish test scope. A practice assessment is not proof that the teacher will reuse its exact questions.

### Verify packet and answer-key relationships

Check matching grade, edition, unit, question numbering, layout, and actual question/answer content before treating a key as a solution to a packet. A common title or module is insufficient. Compare enough content to establish whether the key corresponds to the supplied questions; inspect images when extraction loses the structure.

If they do not match, cite concrete examples and describe the confirmed mismatch. Possible explanations include missing practice pages, a separate review set, or a mistaken attachment, but do not assert an attachment error without evidence. Recommend a focused teacher clarification about the intended packet/key relationship.

For worked examples, accurately reproduce or paraphrase the provided problem, cite it, show the steps, and check the answer independently where feasible. If the school key conflicts with arithmetic or the question, report both the source answer and the checked reasoning; do not silently rewrite the source. Label any newly created practice example as assistant-created and tied to the documented skill.

## Project and assignment record

For each substantial assignment, extract where supplied:

- Title, student, subject, and educational objective.
- Whether it is graded or summative; use "not stated" where unsupported.
- Due date and confirmation status.
- Allowed completion formats and meaningful choices or limitations.
- Required components, expected school work and home work.
- Planning sheets, research/reference materials, teacher instructions, and grading rubric.

If no deadline is supplied, state exactly: **"Deadline not provided in the supplied materials."** Do not infer a due date from the newsletter week, project introduction, or a different assessment. Recommend checking with the teacher when the omission affects planning. A lack of explicit home-work instructions does not prove the project must be done at home.

### Interpret the rubric

Extract each scoring category, its criteria, available points, and easy-to-miss requirements. Sum points independently and compare with any printed total. If useful, calculate each category's percentage using the supported total and explain how the student can satisfy it. Highlight heavily weighted components without inventing extra criteria.

Distinguish points, performance-level scales, and category maxima. Do not sum every performance level in a rubric as though all could be earned simultaneously. If weighting, alternatives, or totals are unclear, explain the ambiguity and avoid presenting a guessed total or percentage as settled. Preserve the rubric's original claim when flagging an arithmetic inconsistency.

Use a compact table when helpful:

### Support the student

Offer practical parent support: help choose a permitted topic, plan work around an actual deadline, organize resources, use the supplied checklist, review clarity, and check required components against the rubric. Keep research, thinking, and writing with the student. Do not recommend completing the child's assignment for them.

## Clarification wording

When an unresolved discrepancy needs clarification, explain the confirmed mismatch plainly and suggest focused questions. If the user requests a draft, write warmly and without accusation, include the relevant context, and ask the alternatives the evidence supports. Do not send it without explicit authorization.
