# Source Strategies

Use this as a coverage checklist, not as a claim that every locality has every source type. Search the public web only; do not bypass access controls, impersonate residents, or imply access to nonpublic systems.

## Source families

### Local reporting

- Daily and weekly newspapers, local TV newsrooms, radio news, public media, wire-service local bureaus, and regional desks.
- Hyperlocal sites, neighborhood publications, nonprofit newsrooms, alternative papers, local newsletters, and community-language media.
- University or student newspapers when they regularly cover the locality.
- Local business journals, development publications, real-estate reporting, and credible trade outlets for local employers or infrastructure.

Open each outlet's local, breaking, crime/public safety, government, education, business, weather, traffic, and community pages when they exist. Inspect the latest-posted list as well as search results because search indexing lags.

### City and local-government sources

- Municipal newsrooms, press releases, public notices, open-data portals, alert systems, calendars, and official social accounts.
- Mayor/city manager, council agendas and packets, meeting videos/minutes, clerk records, ordinances, elections, procurement, and budget notices.
- Planning, zoning, landmarks, building permits, code enforcement, economic development, utilities, public works, sanitation, parks, libraries, airports, transit, and housing authorities.
- Local or regional public-health agencies and emergency-management offices.

Agenda publication, packet revision, permit decision, official vote, closure, service interruption, boil-water notice, and project milestone can each be newsworthy even without a conventional article. Routine agenda items should follow the user's public-events preference.

### Public safety and justice

- Police/sheriff news releases, incident alerts, blotters, crime maps, missing-person notices, official social accounts, and publicly queryable calls-for-service/CAD data.
- Fire, EMS, emergency-management, evacuation, burn-ban, hazardous-material, and disaster-response sources.
- Jail/booking search, court dockets, clerk portals, prosecutor and public-defender announcements, and state/federal court sources when locally relevant.

For U.S. records, search city police plus county sheriff, jail, district/county clerk, district attorney, state court portal, and the appropriate federal district. PACER or other paid/login-only access is a blind spot unless the session already has authorized access. A booking is not a conviction; a charge or affidavit is an allegation. Avoid unnecessary personal details from raw records.

Calls-for-service and scanner-like data are not equivalent to confirmed incidents. Preserve the source's terminology, note that calls may be canceled or reclassified, and place uncorroborated items in the raw/unverified section.

### Schools, universities, and institutions

- School-district news and alerts, board agendas, closures, safety notices, athletics only when newsworthy, and state education actions.
- Colleges and universities: central newsroom, campus police/emergency alerts, student media, governing-board material, construction, research, and major events.
- Hospitals and health systems, major nonprofits, cultural institutions, chambers, convention/visitor bureaus, and large local employers when they publish consequential local developments.

### Business and development

- Planning and zoning cases, building/demolition permits, tax-increment or incentive materials, property transactions, utility connections, alcohol/business licenses, and procurement awards.
- Openings, closures, layoffs, expansions, bankruptcies, major leases, and infrastructure projects reported by direct company sources, filings, or credible local outlets.
- Chamber and economic-development announcements are useful but may be promotional; corroborate material claims when possible.

### Weather, traffic, utilities, and incidents

- National or official meteorological service, local emergency management, flood gauges, storm reports, air-quality agencies, and utility outage/alert pages.
- National/state/provincial and local transportation departments, road closures, crash alerts, transit disruptions, airport notices, rail incidents, and major event traffic plans.
- In the U.S., check the National Weather Service office serving the locality, state DOT/511, local traffic/transit agencies, utility outage maps, and FEMA/state emergency sources when relevant.

Routine forecasts are not events. Include warnings, observed severe weather, records, damage, unusual conditions, and material service or travel effects.

### Community and social reporting

- Verified official accounts for agencies and institutions, plus attributable posts from local reporters.
- Neighborhood associations, mutual-aid groups, community organizations, local forums, Reddit, and public social posts as lead sources.

Label self-reports, anonymous posts, photographs without verified time/location, and rumors as unverified. Search for corroboration by an agency, direct witness with attributable evidence, or credible newsroom. Do not use engagement volume as verification.

### Aggregators and archives

- News aggregators, RSS feeds, local-news aggregation sites, web/search news tabs, newsletters, cached pages, and web archives can reveal missed or removed items.
- Treat them as discovery layers. Cite the underlying source when accessible and identify duplicates or syndication.

## Search patterns

Adapt queries to the locality and language. Useful patterns include:

- `LOCATION news`, then date filter and freshness sorting;
- `LOCATION police OR fire OR crash OR closure OR outage OR arrest`;
- `LOCATION council OR agenda OR notice OR permit OR zoning OR development`;
- `LOCATION school OR university OR hospital OR business`;
- `site:official-domain LOCATION` plus dates or department terms;
- neighborhood, highway, landmark, agency, and county names within the window;
- alternate spellings, local-language names, abbreviations, demonyms, and transliterations;
- a distinctive phrase from one candidate to find the primary source and independent corroboration.

Search a buffer before and after the exact window, but classify entries by the requested boundaries rather than the search filter's coarse date labels.

## U.S.-specific discovery

When applicable, look for:

- official `.gov`, state/local open-data, agenda-management, public-notice, and records portals;
- city/county alert platforms and official Facebook/X/Instagram/YouTube posts when their websites lag;
- police/fire CAD, calls-for-service, incident maps, jail rosters, state court search, county clerk, state public-safety, and state fire-marshal sources;
- NWS forecast office and Local Storm Reports, NOAA products, state DOT/511, transit and airport alerts;
- FCC-licensed local broadcasters, public radio, nonprofit newsrooms, community newspapers, and non-English local media;
- school districts, community colleges, public/private universities, and their police/alert systems.

Availability and disclosure rules differ sharply by state and agency. Record a portal's freshness and query limitations; do not infer that an empty or inaccessible portal means nothing happened.

## Fallbacks outside the United States

Map U.S. categories to the local system rather than searching for U.S. agency names:

- municipality/local authority, regional/provincial government, and national government portals;
- national or regional police, fire/rescue, ambulance, civil-protection, and judicial systems;
- national meteorological, geological, transport, road, rail, aviation, and utility operators;
- public broadcasters, local-language newspapers, community radio, and neighborhood media;
- schools, universities, hospitals, chambers, and major institutions.

Search in the dominant local language(s) and English when useful. Use translated terms, alternate scripts, and transliterations. If automated translation is necessary, say so; preserve proper nouns and distinguish machine-translated interpretation from quoted official terminology. Where court, jail, incident, agenda, or public-record access is not customary or lawful, mark that family unavailable rather than substituting rumor.

## Coverage record

Maintain a compact internal matrix with one row per source family: sources checked, latest visible timestamp, candidates found, and limitation. The final answer need not expose the full matrix unless useful, but its unresolved limitations must appear under blind spots.
